## 🎯 What Worked Well

### 1. Engine-first, test-driven — build the testable correctness core before the untestable UI
The whole feature rests on a pure `organizeEngine.js` `consolidate` mode: `sourceFolderId: '__all__'`, destination-subtree exclusion (never flatten/reshuffle what's home), per-book removal from its *actual* folders, and `consolidateRemovals` for the per-membership pick. All of it landed **first**, locked by 4 new unit tests (12 → **16**), and validated in Node before a line of preview code changed. When the UI (which I can't run) had to change, the hard logic was already proven.

### 2. The three-state model unified the whole preview
Ron's "I want to see *all* of Lee Child, wherever it is" pushed us to show the author's **full footprint**, which resolved into three states — **mover / already-here / elsewhere** — with distinct visual languages (raised+outlined / solid recessed tray / dashed tray + location tag). It's not a bolt-on: it **degrades cleanly** (consolidate touches everything → there's simply no "elsewhere"), so one model covers narrow-folder, Inbox, and All-Books scopes.

### 3. The Scope toggle unified the two entry points
"Can I switch Prime(able) → All Books inside the dialog?" turned the folder-Auto-Organize and the All-Books-Consolidate into **one dialog at two scope settings** — flip in place (like By Author/Series), and you're still standing in the folder when you're done. The Elsewhere tray *set this up*: it shows "5 more in Inbox," and the toggle is the one click that acts on it. Feature emerged from feature.

### 4. A small data-model insight unlocked a "rare-case" feature cheaply
The per-copy pick looked like a big selection refactor. The real change was Ron's: **key the selection on `(folder, book)`, not just `book`**. Kept as an additive `autoOrgExcludedMembers` set (consolidate-only), it left every non-consolidate path untouched and reused the existing cover/selection machinery.

### 5. Ron's UX instinct repeatedly beat the first pass
- **Rejected primary-author parsing** for the compound `Grady Hendrix, Moniqua Plante, Amazon Original Stories` case — it'd mis-file Niven/Pournelle collaborations and reopen the author-parsing swamp we'd sworn off. Kept hygiene whitespace-only; compound-author books get filed by hand (which the new menu filter makes painless).
- **Refused to defer** the per-copy pick to a TODO (avoid tech debt) — correct, it was a clean in-branch change.
- **Discovered "Open" was a non-issue** in the organizer: the checklist toggle model means a double-click toggles off→on (net zero) and opens — so no redundant menu item needed.

## ❌ Mistakes Made

### 1. Proposed reopening a settled decision (author parsing)
I floated "match on the first author before the comma" to make the compound-author audiobook visible. Ron caught it: comma-in-author is ambiguous (co-authors vs author+narrator+imprint), and first-author matching breaks his deliberate Niven/Pournelle collaboration folders. **Lesson: before reopening a settled call, recall *why* it was settled** — I had the counterexample in the history and still proposed it.

### 2. Defaulted to deferring (again)
My instinct was "ship whole-book consolidate now, add the per-copy pick later." Ron pushed back on the tech-debt TODO; the change was small once the `(folder,book)` framing was clear. The 6.16.0 lesson ("minor became major") has a twin: **don't over-defer the finish, either** — sometimes the right-sized-now beats the split.

### 3. "Minor" became "major" — again
Started as "fix the Prime duplicate bug." Became a 5-alpha, library-wide Consolidate feature (all-source engine, three-state preview, per-copy pick, scope toggle). Every step was agreed and the result is coherent, but the initial framing was, once more, optimistic.

### 4. A real gap surfaced but wasn't the headline
The compound-author book is **invisible to Auto-Organize** (both the mover logic and already-filed detection walk the exact-author group), which is *why* it wasn't offered for filing. That's a genuine gap (logged under author hygiene), distinct from the cover-flicker it was first noticed alongside.

## 📚 Lessons Learned

1. **Engine-first + unit tests** for the correctness core; wire the locally-untestable in-browser JSX *after*, incrementally, with the user testing each alpha.
2. **Don't reopen a settled decision** without the reasons it was settled in hand (author parsing).
3. **"Show me everything" beats narrow scope** — the full-footprint three-state model unified the preview and made the scope toggle obvious.
4. **A small model insight can make a "rare" feature cheap** — `(folder,book)` selection; don't reflexively TODO it.
5. **Check whether an existing gesture already does the job** (double-click preserving selection via the toggle model) before adding a redundant affordance.

## 🤖 AI Collaboration Insights

### What Worked
- **Design-confirm-before-build on the semantically load-bearing pieces** — the `(folder,book)` selection model, the three-state visual language, and the scope-toggle behavior were each restated and agreed before code. That's the [[feedback_confirm_operation_semantics]] discipline paying off.
- **Committing an alpha before each test** kept a clean, revertible trail across 5 iterations, and the release was a trivial fast-forward.

### Friction
- **No local browser test** (in-browser Babel/JSX): every preview change verified by Ron. Mitigated by (a) the pure engine + Node tests for all correctness, and (b) careful read-before-edit on the ~17k-line file.
- **Big edits on one giant file** remain the main risk surface; the elsewhere + per-copy render touched the most intricate part (slot building). Traced container open/close before wrapping.

## Final Thoughts
This began as a one-line bug ("organizing from All Books left a duplicate") and, by following the *right* principle each step — handle-don't-forbid, show-the-whole-picture, one-surface-for-everything, and a small data-model fix over a deferred TODO — became **library-wide Consolidate**. The pure engine + tests carried the correctness; Ron's workflow instinct carried the design (full footprint, scope toggle, per-copy pick, no author parsing). The two separate entry points (folder-organize, All-Books-consolidate) are now one dialog with a scope switch.

**Key Takeaway**: When a bug's real fix is "the tool should show and act on the whole picture," build the picture — and let a small selection-model insight (`(folder,book)`) deliver the fine control instead of deferring it.
