# Post-Mortem: 7.5.0 — the KV write diet

**Date**: 2026-08-29
**Release**: 7.5.0 prod + dev (151646f, clean fast-forward); worker prod-deployed first (1615f889)

The whole arc ran alert → analysis → plan → ship in about a day of work: Cloudflare's 25%/50%
daily-write alerts (with ONE active user) triggered a verified capacity analysis, which produced
three levers logged at the top of TODO, which shipped the next session as one branch, three
layer-ordered commits (worker → client → app), one release. Post-release, the analysis was folded
into RELAY-ECONOMICS.md, closing the long-standing "revisit free-vs-paid with real numbers" item —
the launch decision is now reduced to *flip the Cloudflare plan to Workers Paid ($5/mo ≈ ~250
Ron-like users)*.

---

## ✅ What worked well

1. **Alert-driven prioritization with verified numbers.** The usage emails didn't go to a vague
   "optimize sometime" note — they went through pricing verification and per-user op math first,
   so the three levers were chosen by measured share of the burn (counter ≈ half of all writes;
   double-write ≈ a quarter of push cost; poll = 144 lists/day competing with writes for the same
   pool). No speculative optimization; every lever had a number attached before any code moved.
2. **Layer-ordered commits, each with its own test gate.** Worker (harness), client (harness with
   a flipped assertion), app (Ron's tab-switching eyes). When the harness ran once at the end, it
   validated levers 1+2 together against the dev worker running the new counter — 55/55.
3. **Tests that assert absence.** Lever 2's commit didn't just delete the double-write — it
   flipped the harness assertion to *"legacy key UNTOUCHED by putDeviceState"* (byte-length of the
   seeded legacy value unchanged). Removals regress silently unless a test pins the absence.
4. **The design exceeded the request.** Ron asked to poll less; the event-driven return-poll
   (instant check on tab/window focus regain) made the banner *faster* at the exact moment it
   matters — returning from the Amazon fetch tab — while cutting background checks ~90%.
   "It was not my intent. It is better than my intent."
5. **Live testing surfaced the observability gap immediately.** Ron couldn't tell whether polls
   were firing — one alpha later, every poll logs its trigger (`tab return` / `heartbeat`) and its
   result. Cheap, and it converted the rest of the test session from guesswork to observation.
6. **The "missing import" was resolved without touching code.** Two candidate stories (letter
   never sent vs. letter discarded) with three discriminating questions split them cleanly; the
   answer was a June-3 duplicate the fetcher had correctly refused — both experiments explained
   by one fact. The system was right; the discipline was not assuming it was wrong.

## ❌ What went wrong

1. **Lever 1's first draft had an enforcement hole.** The initial sampling structure early-returned
   on non-sampled requests, skipping the over-limit check — a hot channel would leak 4 of every 5
   writes past the cap. Self-caught before commit and restructured. The durable lesson: **sampling
   may only gate the *persist*, never the *enforcement*** — the check must evaluate every request
   against the (approximate) persisted count.
2. **The first banner test was doubly confounded, and one confound was ours to anticipate.** Ron's
   test book turned out to be a June-3 duplicate (cockpit error, his call) — but the test script I
   gave him also ignored the known ~60s KV list lag: "add a book, switch back" *cannot* show a
   banner even when everything works, because the return-poll fires seconds before the letter is
   listable. The retest instruction ("stay away 90+ seconds") should have been in the original
   checklist. Known platform lags belong in test scripts, not in the debrief.
3. **The fetcher's duplicate notice didn't land.** The overlay said "Already on your wishlist" and
   Ron missed it — which cost the first experiment and seeded the "importer is discarding books???"
   scare. No change made (the overlay is the designed surface, and the console said it plainly),
   but it's a data point: for a bookmarklet whose overlay auto-dismisses, a *refusal* is easy to
   read as a *success*. If this recurs, consider making the duplicate outcome visually distinct
   from the success outcome (color/icon), not just different words.

## 🔧 Process notes

- **Worst-case banner latency is now ~21 minutes** (20-min focused heartbeat + list lag) for a
  user who adds a book and *never leaves the app tab* — accepted deliberately, since running a
  fetch means being in an Amazon tab, and the return trip fires the instant check. A one-shot
  follow-up recheck ~2 min after an empty return-poll was considered and **not** adopted (no
  observed need; another tab-flip already covers it). Revisit only if real usage complains.
- **Doc fold-in same-day**: the capacity analysis went into RELAY-ECONOMICS.md in the same sitting
  as the release, while the numbers were still warm — the TODO item died instead of aging.
- README/features.html deliberately not updated: infrastructure release, consistent with 7.1–7.4.

## 👀 Watch

- **Cloudflare usage-alert emails over the next few days.** With all three levers live, daily
  write consumption should drop visibly. If the 25% alert still fires with one active user,
  something else is burning writes — investigate, don't shrug.

## Final thought

This is what the 7.0 architecture was for: a cost problem arrived, and the response was three
surgical commits in three layers, each independently testable, shipped in a day — because the
commit pattern, the harness, and the layer boundaries already existed. The one near-miss (the
enforcement hole) reinforces the sprint's standing theme: optimizations that touch a guard must
prove the guard still guards.
