Five releases in two days of work, bracketing a week's diversion. The through-line: everything 7.0 built kept paying — atomic commits made the device-state fix a copy-adapt, letters made checkpointing thinkable, and the harness absorbed every new subsystem.

---

## ✅ What worked well

1. **Measure before deciding.** The device-state 25 MB question sat as a design-doc `[verify]` for weeks; one console snippet produced *16.98 MB = 67.9% full* — and Phase 1b went from "someday" to **promoted, built, and shipped within 30 hours**. The number did the prioritizing; nobody had to argue.
2. **Reader-before-writer as a *versioning* exercise.** Because mobile reads device-state through the shared relay-client, the dual-format reader shipped with **zero mobile.js changes** — the two-release discipline (7.2.0 reader, 7.3.0 writer, legacy double-write bridging) closed every cached-session edge without a flag day.
3. **A spec review that killed its own spec.** RESTORE-SAFEGUARD's keep/merge design dissolved under Ron's own scenario analysis (merge resurrects deliberate deletions; double-places moved books); the shipped informed-confirm + back-up-current-first is *simpler and safer* than what was specced. The rejection is recorded in the rewritten doc — and paid off the same day, when Ron re-asked the merge question and the paper answered.
4. **Recorded rejections generally**: "Cloud Relay" naming, the price-date column, the exact-match restore diff, per-book adaptive TTLs — each has a tombstone with reasons. Re-litigation cost this sprint: zero.
5. **Live-fire testing culture.** Ron tested the 12 MB pretend-limit journal split with his real 46.7 MB payload; and when the reset-confirm dialog needed testing, he **pulled the trigger on a real wipe** — converting the riskiest untested path (empty-system full restore) into a verified one. Backups you've restored are the only backups you have.
6. **Calibration by experiment**: the dimming opacity was judged imperceptible only after Ron moved the Price column next to a populated one to *create contrast for judging* — instrumentation instinct worth naming.

## ❌ What went wrong

1. **Scripted-edit escape collapse — three bites, escalating.** Multi-line python-heredoc edits with backslash escapes (`\n`, `’`) collapsed in transport three separate times: (a) truncated `series-page-fetcher.js` to 0 bytes (restored from git; adopted temp+atomic-replace), (b) injected a **literal newline into a JS string, taking the whole app down** — Ron landed on the Reset screen and nearly pressed the button, (c) died silently mid-script so **the alpha.8 commit claimed 8 edits and contained none** (only the version bump) — a commit message that lied. **Rule now standing: content containing backslash escapes goes through the literal-safe Edit tool, never heredoc scripting.** Three-for-three earned a ban, not a caution.
2. **Fabricated timestamps.** The response-header timestamp rule ("run Get-Date") got quietly optimized into extrapolation from stale context — drifting 4½ hours — and then **corrupted an analysis**: Ron was told the 24-hour staleness line hadn't crossed when it had. He caught it from the randomness of the headers. The failure shape matters: *a compliance ritual degraded into fiction, and the fiction fed a wrong conclusion*. Tool-invoked time every response, no arithmetic-from-memory — and a memory entry so the lesson outlives this session.
3. **Missed TODO sweep at release** — the original Restore-safeguard Pre-Launch item survived the 7.4.0 release commit; caught and swept post-push. The checklist's sweep step needs to check *shipped-feature* items, not just `[x]` marks.
4. **The wrong-instance footgun** — Ron twice operated on prod believing it was localhost (identical tabs). Fixed with the LOCAL badge + his own prod-in-dark-mode convention; the deeper lesson is that *indistinguishable environments invite cross-environment mistakes*, and labels are cheaper than any recovery.

## 🔧 Process notes

- **Vocabulary rule established**: "Relay" = proper noun on its named surfaces; "cloud" = destination prose everywhere else. Pre-launch **"Cloud Sync" rename** logged (Cloud Relay explicitly rejected) — renames are free before launch, expensive forever after.
- **Presentation-cluster restore rule**: backups contain everything; empty-system restores apply everything (incl. theme, added this release); rollbacks keep the current presentation. The convention pair that resolved it: full-device restores bring settings; data-level restores never touch preferences.
- **Ron's 24h staleness override** (vs. my proposed 30d) was correct: sale prices turn daily, and "all prices dim between fetches" is honest, not noisy.

The 7.0 foundation is compounding: this sprint's biggest feature (killing the 25 MB ceiling) was mostly *reusing* the commit pattern, and its biggest safety win (trustworthy restore) shipped simpler than its own spec. The failures that mattered were all **mechanical honesty** failures — edits that didn't land, a commit that overstated, timestamps that were invented — and every one was caught by Ron or by a check, never by luck.
