## 🎯 What Worked Well

### 1. **Prove a reusable primitive on ONE instance before mass-converting**
- `FlipToFitPopup` (measured submenu positioning) was built and applied to **just the Share submenu** (alpha.2), browser-tested, *then* rolled out to the other seven. When the horizontal flip turned out inverted, it surfaced on that one submenu with a clean signal ("vertical works, horizontal doesn't, the un-converted ones flip fine") that pinned it to the primitive, not the conversions.
- **Lesson**: for a shared component you'll apply N times, validate N=1 in the real app first.

### 2. **Read the risky step before budgeting/surgery**
- The Auto-Organize reorg was blocked by a nested IIFE splitting the menu's scope. Reading it first revealed the fix (hoist `selectedBooksArray`/`count`, drop the IIFE) instead of guessing — and made the reorg a clean follow-on.

### 3. **Principle-driven UX, driven hard by the user**
- The sharpest calls came from naming principles: **pick-list vs form** (why Tags/Price stay top-level, Edit's fields don't), **consequence-gradient ordering** (placement → reference → edit → delete), **Owned vs borrowed** (the terminology upgrade), and **prominence = frequency × scope** (why Ownership stays tucked in Edit). Most originated or got sharpened by Ron. Capturing them in BOOK-CONTEXT-MENU.md is the durable payoff.

### 4. **Corrected my own wrong claim by checking code**
- I twice implied a manual owned⇄wishlist edit would be overwritten by the next fetch. Ron pushed ("wouldn't it be overwritten?"), I checked storage.js + the edit paths, found the `userEdited.onWishlist` protection, and reversed the recommendation. The conclusion flipped from "maybe remove it" to "keep it — it's durable and serves manual-add books."

## ❌ Mistakes Made

### 1. **Shipped a broken alpha (JSX fragment across a container boundary)**
- alpha.12 wrapped the Folders "Inbox + tree" in `{!foldersSectionCollapsed && (<>…</>)}`, but the Inbox lives inside the *header's* container while the tree is a separate `<div className="p-2">`. The fragment straddled that boundary → `Expected corresponding JSX closing tag for <>`. Ron hit the syntax error on reload.
- **Root cause**: I opened the wrapper and placed the close ~650 lines away *without tracing where the intervening containers open/close*. I assumed a flat sibling structure.
- **Fix (alpha.13)**: gate **only** the folder-tree IIFE (`{!foldersSectionCollapsed && (() => {…})()}`) — a self-contained expression. Bonus: the Inbox stays visible with the header (better UX).
- **Lesson**: before wrapping a span of JSX in a conditional, **trace the open/close of every container inside that span**. A fragment must not cross a `<div>` boundary.

### 2. **Over-asserted a data-persistence behavior (twice) before verifying**
- The "ownership edits get overwritten" claim was stated confidently and was wrong. Cost: a couple of exchanges and a reversed recommendation.
- **Lesson**: claims about "does the fetch/import overwrite this field?" are checkable in code (`userEdited` + the merge). Verify *before* asserting, especially when steering a design decision.

### 3. **"Quick wins" scope estimate was wildly off**
- The batch was pitched as small (#1–#4). #2 (menu reorg) alone spawned a positioning-primitive refactor, a scope-unification, and the Edit rework; ownership spawned an app-wide terminology sweep. 14 alphas, not "a few one-liners." The user was on board at each step, but the framing was optimistic.

## 📚 Lessons Learned

### 1. **Trace container open/close before wrapping JSX** (the alpha.12 bug — cheapest to prevent, hit production).
### 2. **Verify persistence/behavior claims in code before asserting** (the ownership reversal).
### 3. **Measured DOM positioning beats pixel estimates** for any menu with positional coupling — it removed a whole brittleness class *and* fixed a latent cut-off bug the estimates had.
### 4. **Lean into the user's domain intuition** — "pick-list vs form," "Owned vs borrowed," "the decision should be the selection not the contents" (a self-corrected false alarm, but the instinct was right to check). The user's framings were repeatedly sharper than my first pass.

## Final Thoughts
This started as "knock out some quick wins" and became the deepest UX pass on the book menu and sidebar we've done — because we kept finding the *right* underlying principle instead of the tactical patch (measure don't estimate; pick-list vs form; Owned vs borrowed; consequence-gradient order). The one production stumble (a JSX fragment across a container boundary) and the one reversed claim (ownership persistence) share a root: acting before fully reading the structure/behavior. Cheap to prevent, and the fixes made the result better.

**Key Takeaway**: Read the structure before you wrap it, and verify the behavior before you assert it — the two mistakes this release both trace to skipping that step.
